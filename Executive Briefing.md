## 1. Executive Summary & Bottom Line Up Front

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department (PID) approved average rate increases of **15.97% for the individual market** and **10.28% for the small group market** for Plan Year 2027, successfully denying **$94.7 million** in carrier-requested premium increases. 

Independent forensic verification—cross-referencing primary Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) risk-adjustment data—confirms the underlying actuarial basis for rate adjustments while resolving several critical data discrepancies:

* **Morbidity Verifications:** CMS 2025 Benefit Year data confirms that all three above-request rate approvals were justified by sicker enrollee pools (Keystone Health Plan East received **+$41.4M**, Health Partners Plans received **+$24.8M**, and QCC Insurance received **+$3.7M** in net risk-adjustment transfers).
* **Largest Risk-Adjustment Recipient:** Ambetter Health / Pennsylvania Health & Wellness received **+$61.1M** in net transfers, confirming the highest morbidity pool in the Commonwealth.
* **Enrollment Attrition Correction:** Public claims alleging a "1 in 3" (33%) coverage drop were incorrect due to conflating gross cancellations with net retention. Actual marketplace net attrition stands at **16%** (497,000 peak plan selections vs. 417,158 paid effectuated enrollees).
* **Critical Unannounced Pressures:** The audit identified key hidden cost drivers, including Ambetter’s complete phase-out of Bronze tier plans (forcing members into higher-cost Silver plans) and an unfunded **$600+ million** state reinsurance backfill.

## 3. Pennie Enrollment Trajectory & Metric Reconciliation

Previous reporting conflated gross enrollment cancellations during Open Enrollment (~85,000) with net attrition. The trajectory below reconciles the enrollment counts across standardized metric definitions:

```
[Dec 31, 2025]  Peak Plan Selections:        497,000 enrollees
[Jan 31, 2026]  Close of Open Enrollment:    486,000 enrollees (-11,000)
[Mar 24, 2026]  Active Enrollment:           471,442 enrollees (-25,558)
[Sep 30, 2026]  Paid Effectuated Enrollment: 417,158 enrollees (-79,842 net loss)
```

**Key Takeaway:** Net retention stands at **84.0%** (a 16.0% net decline from peak). Coverage losses are concentrated among households at 150%–200% FPL and individuals aged 55–64 due to age-band pricing (3:1 ratio).

## 4. Public Consumer Guidance & Action Items

### What Consumers Need to Know
1. **Rates Vary Significantly by Carrier:** While the statewide average increase is 15.97%, actual rate changes depend heavily on your plan provider. Oscar Health (+1.38%), Geisinger (+8.57%), and UPMC (+9.73%) received significantly lower rate increases than Ambetter (+35.13%) or Keystone Central (+33.39%).
2. **Ambetter Bronze Plan Discontinuation:** Ambetter is phasing out all Bronze plans for 2027. Enrollees currently on Ambetter Bronze plans will be automatically migrated to Silver plans, resulting in effective premium increases that exceed 35%.
3. **Immigrant Subsidy Changes:** Effective January 1, 2027, federal policy updates restrict Advance Premium Tax Credits (APTC) to Lawfully Permanent Residents. An estimated 20,000 to 30,000 lawfully present Pennsylvanians (refugees, asylees, and trafficking survivors) will lose eligibility for financial assistance.

## 5. Media & Regulatory Policy Q&A

**Q: Why did the PID approve rate increases above carrier requests for three insurers?**  
*Answer:* Federal risk-adjustment data from CMS confirmed that these carriers enrolled sicker patient pools than anticipated, creating higher liability. To prevent insolvency and preserve carrier choice, PID approved adjusted requests: Keystone East received $41.4M in CMS transfers, Health Partners received $24.8M, and QCC received $3.7M.

**Q: Is insurer profit padding driving rate increases?**  
*Answer:* Primary data indicates otherwise. According to KFF benchmarks, individual market carriers spent 93% of premium dollars directly on medical claims in 2025 (93% Loss Ratio). Furthermore, PID explicitly capped carrier profit margins at 1.0% in decisions where excess padding was suspected.

**Q: Why did Oscar Health receive a near-zero rate approval (1.38%)?**  
*Answer:* Oscar's low approval rate reflects small-sample mechanics rather than regulatory favoritism. Oscar entered PY2026 with a baseline of only 1,347 members in Pennsylvania, making its risk pool less representative of broader statewide trends.
